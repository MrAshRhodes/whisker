# Changelog

All notable changes to Whisker are documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/),
and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- Two-button chords: press two buttons of the same mouse together to run an action, set up in the Two-button Chords section of Settings › Buttons with Record Two-button Chord… (recording runs no action) or the Primary + Secondary → Middle Click preset. Chords work for a mouse connected over Bluetooth while Input Monitoring is allowed and no other connected mouse is the same model; identical models, USB mice, receivers and presses that cannot be attributed pass through unchanged, and adding or recording is unavailable until such a mouse is ready, while saved chords can always be edited or removed. A single press of a chord's button waits up to the press window (75 ms unless changed, 20–200 ms) and then runs its normal mapping; other buttons are never delayed. A chord using the primary button is blocked unless Allow remapping the primary button is on, so the preset adds ⌥ when it is off. The action carries held modifier keys, and the middle-click chord sends one middle down on recognition, supports dragging and releases on the first button let go. App switches, settings changes, recording, disconnect, sleep, quitting and event-tap interruptions cancel a chord in progress. A press attributed more than about 30 ms late passes through, any movement abandons a waiting chord, chords pass through while buttons are swapped in macOS settings, and it has not yet been verified on hardware ([#24]).
- Extra button discovery (stage 1, opt-in diagnostic): inspect a selected mouse's collections and Button/Consumer elements in Devices, labelled observed, unsupported or ambiguous without inventing CGEvent button numbers. Input Monitoring is required even with one mouse. Only safely attributable binary elements on one serial-identified direct Bluetooth interface can supply filtered values; USB devices and receivers remain metadata-only without proven association. Keyboard interfaces remain metadata-only, primary-button values and excluded touch devices are untouched, and discovery sends no reports or device writes. Bounded observations include releases and clear on disable, app/profile/settings changes, sleep, permission loss and reconnect. Ordinary mappings are unchanged; separate additive settings reserve future extra-button identities without executing mappings. No vendor adapter or model/firmware/transport is approved or hardware-verified; stage 2 remapping, native-effect suppression and crash recovery remain unavailable ([#25]).
- Click Lock (experimental, off by default): turn on Enable Click Lock in Settings › General › Experimental, then give a button the Click Lock action in Buttons. Pressing that button once holds the primary button down for you, so moving the mouse drags, and pressing any button given Click Lock, on any mouse, lets go; letting go of the button itself does nothing. The button has to be a mouse button other than the primary button, and it cannot be the wheel. You can always get out by pressing the primary button on any mouse (the lock lets go first and the press then goes through as normal), with a button or wheel direction set to click the primary button (it lets go instead of clicking again), or with Release Click Lock at the top of the menu bar menu, which is there only while it is held. While it is held the menu bar icon becomes a padlock, even if the icon is hidden, and its tooltip says Click Lock is on. It lets go once, by itself, on a change of app, bypass, profile or settings, removing its mapping, Whisker being switched off, losing Accessibility access or macOS pausing an event tap, sleep, quitting, recording a button, or the mouse that began it being unplugged. It does not start, and the press is ignored, while another button is down, Hold to Scroll or a button gesture is under way, the pointer-movement listener it needs is not ready, or the duplicate-click filter held the press back. Click Lock is not offered for button gestures, and while it is held auto-scroll and gestures cannot start. It needs Accessibility but no Input Monitoring of its own. With the switch off a Click Lock mapping does nothing, and settings from earlier versions load and save unchanged. It has not yet been verified on hardware: drag-selecting in TextEdit, moving and dragging in Finder, recovering after Accessibility access is revoked or Whisker is force-quit while it is held (if the button stays down, press and release the primary button yourself), whether apps always see the lock's release before the primary press that follows it, and use with Hold to Scroll, several displays or mixed pointing devices are all untried ([#19]).
- Input timing (opt-in diagnostic): Input Timing… beside a connected mouse in Settings › Devices counts when that mouse's HID reports reach Whisker over a ten-second sample, while you move it in steady circles. For each interface and report ID, shown apart and never added together, it gives how many reports arrived, about how many a second and the gaps between them (shortest, median, 95th percentile and longest), and says plainly when there was too little movement, nothing arrived or the timestamps did not line up. Nothing is read until you press Start. It needs Input Monitoring only for the sample, records when each report arrived and never what was in it, listens without taking the device over and writes nothing to it, and saves nothing: cancelling, closing the window, sleep, a lost permission, or the mouse being unplugged or changing stops it and discards the sample. Trackpads, touch mice, keyboard interfaces and identical mice without serial numbers are not timed. It counts reports received, not the mouse's polling rate, latency, packet loss or a game's input rate, and it has not yet been verified on hardware, wired or with a wireless receiver ([#13]).
- Button gestures (opt-in): turn on Gestures for a button in Buttons and give it up to five once-only actions, one each for holding the button while you move the mouse up, down, left or right, and an optional Tap for letting go without having moved. The first movement that adds up to the distance you choose (60 to start, from 20 to 400, in the movement macOS reports) and runs at least twice as far along one axis as across it picks the direction; a diagonal, or jitter that goes nowhere, picks nothing. Letting go runs the action once, the app sees no click or drag from that button, and other buttons, other mappings and an unmodified primary click carry on as usual. It needs Input Monitoring and an exactly identified mouse, only the mouse you pressed counts, and a change of app, a lost mouse, changed settings, Whisker switched off, sleep or quitting calls the gesture off with nothing run. Settings from earlier versions load and save unchanged. It works with buttons macOS reports as ordinary presses and releases; the MX Master 3S gesture button is not yet verified, no mouse is listed as supported, buttons macOS does not report are out of reach, and the distances and rules are provisional and not yet verified on hardware ([#15]).
- Auto-scroll (experimental): press a button you choose to anchor the pointer, let go, and the window under that spot keeps scrolling, faster the further you move the pointer from the anchor (down below it, up above it, and sideways as well with Both Directions). The dead zone, from 4 to 120 points, and the top speed, from 100 to 6,000 pixels a second, are settings, and the speed follows elapsed time rather than the number of events. Pressing the same button again, pressing any other mouse button (which then does what it normally would), turning the wheel or swiping a trackpad, a change of app, bypass, profile, settings, Space or display, a mouse being connected or disconnected, sleep, a user switch, quitting, losing Accessibility access or macOS pausing the event tap stops it, and it never starts again by itself. There is no marker at the pointer; the menu bar icon changes and its tooltip says auto-scroll is on, even when the icon is hidden. The pointer is never moved and the scrolling is sent to the anchor. It needs Accessibility but no Input Monitoring of its own, its speeds are provisional, and it has not yet been verified on hardware, including in TextEdit, in nested scrolling in Safari, with several displays or with mixed pointing devices ([#18]).
- Slow pointer while held: a spare button temporarily applies a ×0.1 to ×0.9 factor to one exactly identified, supported mouse with a custom pointer baseline. Overlapping holds use the slowest factor without changing saved settings or acceleration; release and a keyboard-accessible cancellation button restore the latest baseline. Lifecycle changes cancel stale holds, failed restoration retains recovery values and is reported, and the primary-button safeguard remains. This changes pointer response, not sensor DPI, and hardware and game compatibility are not yet verified ([#14]).
- Swap primary and secondary buttons for one mouse (Settings › Devices, or that mouse's own profile in Buttons): an opt-in switch, off by default and saved with the mouse, that makes the two buttons trade places as one pair. Only plain presses swap, so clicks with a modifier key held, other buttons, the wheel, other mice and trackpads are left alone, and a press keeps the button it began as until it is released. It needs Allow remapping the primary button, which is never turned on for you, and Input Monitoring so the mouse can be identified exactly. A mapping of the plain Primary Button or Secondary Button in a profile wins over the swap, and Settings says so. Turning it on starts a 60-second trial that reverts unless kept, with Keep and Switch Off buttons that work from the keyboard and VoiceOver. How it combines with the secondary-click setting in System Settings has not yet been verified, apps that read the mouse directly may ignore it, and it has not yet been verified on hardware ([#11]).
- Pointer orientation for a trackball used sideways or upside down: an Orientation section for each mouse in Settings › Pointer turns that device's movement and drags clockwise by a typed angle, a one-degree step or a quarter turn (0°, 90°, 180°, 270°), and Reset goes back to none at once. Any other angle is tried live and goes back after 5 seconds unless kept (Keep is Return, Revert is Escape); only a kept angle is saved to the per-device `rotationDegrees` setting, so quitting or crashing mid-trial leaves the previous one, and closing Settings, sleep, an event-tap pause, switching Whisker off, unplugging the mouse or an import also go back. Calibrate… records four forward strokes from that device alone and proposes a whole-degree angle with its agreement, tried live before Accept, Retry or Cancel. It needs Accessibility and Input Monitoring; movement that cannot be matched exactly to one device, bypassed apps and zero angles pass through unturned, the wheel, buttons and trackpads are never turned, and the pointer stays on a connected display. A dedicated event tap rewrites the deltas and pointer position only while an angle, trial or calibration is active. It has not yet been verified on hardware: whether every app and the cursor follow the rewritten position, raw-input games, mixed-scale displays and long drags are untried, and Hold to Scroll and button gestures still see unturned movement ([#32]).
- Identify tilt-wheel controls in Buttons: a guided, cancellable flow that previews whether a tilt (and the wheel press) arrives as a numbered button or as horizontal scrolling, and saves only what you confirm. Scrolling is never turned into a guessed button, a horizontal scroll mapping is explained as matching other horizontal scrolling from the same mouse, and optional per-mouse control labels are saved only for an exactly identified mouse and never change what a trigger matches. Not yet verified on hardware ([#23]).
- Best-effort wireless mouse battery status in Settings › Devices: a read-only percentage from the mouse's own standard HID battery element (0 to 100 only, with charging shown only when the mouse reports it explicitly), with Not supported, Waiting for a reading, Unavailable, Stale and Disconnected states. It is read once Input Monitoring is allowed, never from the Mac's battery or a UPS, never shown for an ambiguous device such as identical mice without serial numbers or a receiver, kept in memory only, and no mouse or receiver model has been verified yet ([#28]).
- Opt-in duplicate-click filter for one mouse and chosen buttons (Settings › Devices › Duplicate clicks): a press within 20 to 100 ms of the previous click's release that is let go just as quickly is dropped together with its release, while the first click is never delayed and a drag, long hold or other input lets a held press through in order. The wheel and other mice are never filtered, a bare left click stays exempt unless primary-button remapping is allowed, turning it on starts a 60-second trial that reverts unless kept, and a fast deliberate double-click can be ignored too. An optional Count accepted and filtered clicks test shows "N accepted, M filtered" for each filtered button, as one VoiceOver element, with Reset Counts; the counts stay in memory, are never saved or logged, start again when a filter setting changes, while a button is being recorded or when the event tap pauses, and the test ends when the filter is turned off, the mouse disconnects, the Mac sleeps or you leave the page. Click counts stay correct after a dropped repeat, and clicks Whisker sends itself are never filtered. It has not yet been verified on faulty hardware or across apps ([#9]).
- Documented decision: Magic Mouse and other touch mice stay left alone, with no opt-in for their buttons, because clicks cannot yet be told apart from gestures or other mice and no model has been verified on hardware. Native gestures and scrolling are untouched, and a tested eligibility policy records what any future opt-in would need ([#29]).
- Smooth notched scrolling: an opt-in setting for each profile that spreads the distance a wheel notch already scrolls over 40 to 400 milliseconds. It adds no distance and no coasting, covers only notches from an exactly identified mouse, leaves trackpads, smooth-scrolling and touch mice alone, switches off while Reduce Motion is on, and drops anything pending when Whisker is switched off, the Mac sleeps, an app comes to the front or Hold to Scroll starts. It has not yet been verified in real applications ([#27]).
- Smooth sideways scrolling for Scroll Left and Scroll Right mappings: an opt-in mode that runs the action once per swipe from an exactly identified mouse, after an activation distance in pixels, while vertical scrolling carries on. Momentum, trackpads and unidentified input pass through, and it has not yet been verified on hardware, including thumb wheels ([#17]).
- Scroll faster or slower while holding modifier keys: choose the keys, a speed from ×0.25 to ×8 and the directions for each profile. It applies to wheel notches only, not trackpads or smooth scrolling, and has not yet been verified on hardware ([#16]).
- Native wheel speed and acceleration: an opt-in, per-mouse setting that changes the mouse's own wheel values, offered only when Whisker can read and restore them. It suspends Whisker's lines per notch and acceleration for that mouse, puts the original values back when switched off, on quit, in bypass and after a crash, and has not yet been verified on hardware ([#26]).
- Hold to Scroll (experimental): hold a button and roll the trackball to scroll instead of moving the pointer, with axes, speed and direction settings. It needs Input Monitoring, falls back to normal pointer movement when the mouse can't be identified, and has not yet been verified on hardware ([#31]).
- Automatic Axis Lock for Hold to Scroll: an opt-in choice next to the fixed and both-direction modes. Each hold starts undecided, picks vertical or horizontal from its first movement (a balanced diagonal counts as vertical), keeps the first movement on that axis once, drops sideways drift, and starts fresh at the next press. The threshold is provisional, not an ergonomic measurement, and neither it nor thumb versus finger trackballs has been verified on hardware ([#30]).
- Application bypass: while an app on your list is in front (a game, say), Whisker stands aside, with no button remapping, wheel actions, scroll changes or per-mouse pointer speed and acceleration. Anything held is released as the app comes forward, your latest settings return when you switch away, and profiles and the Enabled switch are left as they are. The menu shows "Bypassed for …", and a mouse that won't take its pointer settings back is reported and retried, including at the next launch. ([#12])
- Triple Click action: one press of a mouse button, or one burst of wheel turning, sends three left clicks at the pointer, with optional modifier keys like Double Click. It is a mouse gesture rather than a "select paragraph" command, because what three clicks do is up to the app, and a gesture that is cut short can reach an app as fewer than three clicks. It has not yet been verified in TextEdit, Safari or any other app ([#20]).
- Device details: every device under Settings › Devices has a read-only Details disclosure with its name, connection, vendor and product ids, whether it reports a serial number (shown only when you choose Show), the buttons and wheels it reports to macOS, and why Whisker leaves a device alone or treats several as one. Input Monitoring status is kept separate from how sure Whisker is which mouse a click came from. Nothing new is saved, and reported buttons and wheels are read only once Input Monitoring is allowed and have not yet been verified on hardware ([#10]).
- Pointer speed presets: save a mouse's custom speed under a name, such as "Fast — ×1.5", and switch between that mouse's presets from Settings › Pointer, a per-mouse Pointer Speed submenu in the menu bar menu or the new Pointer Speed Preset button action. A preset changes the software pointer speed only, not acceleration and not the mouse's DPI. A switch that more than doubles the speed uses the same keep-or-revert prompt as the slider (Return keeps it, Esc reverts it, and it goes back after five seconds otherwise), only one change can be waiting at a time, and Use System Settings hands a mouse straight back to macOS. A speed that macOS refuses or only partly takes is rolled back, and if even that is refused the original values are kept and retried. Switching is refused while Whisker or the mouse is off, while the mouse's custom speed is off and while an app on the bypass list is in front, and a mouse that reconnects gets only the last speed you kept. Settings files are now saved as schema version 2, with older files opened unchanged. A speed waiting to be kept is never saved as a setting: it is recorded in pointer-trial.json in Whisker's Application Support folder before it is applied, so if Whisker crashes, is force-quit or the Mac restarts before Keep, the next launch goes back to the last kept speed and says so, and a change that cannot be recorded is refused. It has not yet been verified on hardware ([#21]).
- Unknown settings keys inside nested objects, such as those written by a newer build with the same schema version, now survive a load and save, not just those at the top level. Profiles and speed presets are matched by id, mappings by trigger and modifier keys, control labels by trigger and chords by their two buttons and modifier keys, and the keys of a deleted object do not come back. Keys are still lost for objects that share an identity, for edited shortcut sequence steps and when a known setting changes type, and numbers above 2^53 lose precision ([#14], [#25], [#32]).
- Keyboard Shortcut Sequence action: one press runs two to sixteen shortcuts in order, each with a delay of up to 2,000 ms (10 seconds in total). It never overlaps, the same mapping cancels it, and it is also cancelled from the menu, by a change of frontmost app, changed settings, Whisker being switched off, bypassed, asleep or quit, or lost permission. A step is refused if conflicting modifier keys are held. Delivery is best-effort, with a small race between the focus check and each keystroke, and it has not yet been verified in any real app. Settings move to schema 2, which Whisker 0.1.0 refuses to open ([#22]).

### Fixed

- Make common actions easier to find with grouped choices for Left, Right and Middle Click, Back, Forward and desktop actions. Hide actions a wheel trigger cannot run, explain gesture restrictions, and preserve the button action when toggling gestures ([#36]).
- Create app profiles directly from Buttons and Scrolling, show what the selected profile applies to and inherits, put inherited mappings next to overrides, add Edit Scrolling in Applications, and confirm before deleting an app profile ([#36]).
- Retire slow-pointer holds when their releases lack a fresh device report, so the next hold can slow the pointer again ([#14]).
- Keep overlapping mapped and swapped button presses owned by their originating mice, rather than consuming another mouse's release and leaving a synthetic button held ([#11]).
- Let the primary-button Click Lock escape bypass duplicate-click filtering, including its matching release ([#9], [#19]).
- Reject Hold to Scroll on wheel directions and omit it from wheel action choices instead of silently consuming scrolling ([#31]).
- Cancel superseded battery-refresh timers when details or a device notification request a new reading, keeping one refresh chain per mouse ([#28]).

## [0.1.0] - 2026-11-12

### Added

- Menu bar app for configuring mouse buttons, scrolling and pointer speed.
- Remap any mouse button or wheel direction, with optional modifier keys, to clicks, keyboard shortcuts, Back/Forward, media keys, Mission Control, Spaces, app switching, scrolling or opening an app, file or website.
- Profiles per app and per mouse that inherit mappings from a parent profile.
- Scrolling controls: lines per notch, acceleration, per-mouse reversed direction and a modifier for horizontal scrolling.
- Per-mouse pointer speed and acceleration, restored when Whisker is turned off or quits, with a confirmation step after big changes.
- Button recorder, first-run Accessibility guide, open at login, and settings import, export and reset.
- Settings stored as versioned JSON that is checked and repaired on load (an unreadable file is left untouched and not overwritten); the primary button can't be remapped without a modifier unless allowed.
- Release workflow for signed and notarized builds with opt-in Sparkle in-app updates and a Homebrew cask.
